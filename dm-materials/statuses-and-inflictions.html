Statuses & Inflictions

Mechanical, self-contained triggers built around the Major / Minor / Neutral / Weak Spot tiers.
Statuses
Four umbrella mechanics cover everything. The flavor comes from what caused it and what it's named as when it's applied — not a bespoke rule per name.
StatusEffectClears When
InjuredDowngrade one tier on a specified category of rolls, named when it's appliedWhatever narrative action fits the cause — treated, rested, confronted, or a scene passes
PossessedSkip your next action, or something else controls it instead of youSomeone else snaps you out of it, or a scene passes
BoostedUpgrade one tier on your next rollUsed once, then gone
DespairAny 6 you roll counts as a 5 instead — the best outcome is off the table, no matter what you're rollingA hope-restoring beat
Injured flavors: twisted ankle (footwork), concussion (focus/memory), broken finger (fine motor), burned hand (that hand), bad fall (all physical), winded (costs your bonus die instead of a full tier), fear (the source is present), grief (the loss is invoked), guilt (chose self-preservation over helping), panic (right after a Critical Failure).

Rage is a combo, not its own umbrella: Boosted on Brawns-type rolls and Injured on Common Sense-type rolls, both at once, until the scene ends or it's deliberately calmed.
Bleeding — a stacking status, not a one-time injury: each stack forces one extra "roll 2d6, keep the lowest" die into your next physical roll, stacking additively. Bandaging removes all stacks at once. A Doomsday Prepper's kit clears one stack automatically per scene, no roll needed — they packed for exactly this.
Overwhelmed: if 2+ of the above are active on the same character at once, add a 3rd automatic downgrade on top. Clears once they're back down to 1 or 0 active.
Design Notes
  • Everything has an exit. Every status has a clear way to shed it — players need to feel like they can do something about their condition, not just suffer passively.
  • Bleeding is the death spiral. It's the one status that gets worse if ignored — everything else is a flat, single-tier hit.
  • Overwhelmed is the safety valve. Stack enough of the above — Bleeding, a Flaw trigger, an Injured from a bad fall — and Overwhelmed names that moment explicitly, a great horror beat ("everything is going wrong at once").
  • Name it when you apply it. Injured and Boosted don't come with a fixed list of flavors — when one triggers, just say what it affects (a body part, a fear, a roll type) and move on.
